# Agency relationships in North Carolina (study notes)

Source to verify: North Carolina Real Estate Commission, License Law and Rules, and the Working With Real Estate Agents disclosure.
URLs:
- https://www.ncrec.gov/
- https://www.ncrec.gov/BrochuresAndBulletins

These notes are original study material for a local RAG demo. They are not legal advice and do not reproduce Commission brochures.

## Working With Real Estate Agents

Before a broker begins substantial discussions with a prospective buyer or seller about specific property, North Carolina expects the broker to disclose how the broker will work with that person. The Commission publishes the Working With Real Estate Agents disclosure for this purpose. The consumer should understand whether the broker is a seller's agent, buyer's agent, or something else, and what duties that relationship creates.

## Dual agency

North Carolina allows a firm to represent both the buyer and the seller in the same transaction only with informed consent. Dual agency limits the firm's ability to give undivided loyalty. Brokers generally must not disclose one client's confidential bargaining information (for example, that a seller will take less than list price, or that a buyer will pay more) without authorization.

Written authority for dual agency is required under Commission rules. If a consumer does not consent, the firm cannot lawfully act as a dual agent in that transaction.

## Designated dual agency

A firm may designate one broker to represent the seller and another to represent the buyer in the same in-house transaction, if the firm's procedures and the Commission's rules are followed. Designated agents typically owe their assigned client the duties of a single agent, while the firm remains a dual agent. A broker who already knows confidential information about the other party usually should not be designated.

## Buyer-agent duties (high level)

When working with a buyer as the buyer's agent, a broker owes loyalty, obedience to lawful instructions, disclosure of material facts, confidentiality, accounting, and reasonable skill. Material facts about the property and the transaction must still be disclosed to all parties as required by North Carolina license law.

## Dual compensation / undisclosed compensation

Failing to disclose compensation from more than one party, or receiving an undisclosed rebate, can violate license law (see also G.S. 93A-6). Compensation arrangements should be in writing and disclosed as required.

Always confirm current forms and rules at ncrec.gov before advising a client.
